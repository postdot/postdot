# WEB-162 — Adaptive Week/Day time-grid anchor

## Status

Complete.

## Owner direction

The vertical time grid should respond to the data currently being inspected,
not impose a universal `07:00` working-hours convention. A populated visible
range should open near its first timed publication; an empty range should keep
the existing midnight baseline. The full day remains available in every case.

## What changed

- Added a pure time-anchor helper that ignores all-day events, limits the
  search to the visible range, handles events crossing into the range, respects
  the display timezone, floors to the configured slot interval, and leaves two
  slots of context before the first timed publication.
- Connected the helper to the local Week/Day grid using the dashboard's actual
  vertical scroll container. The effect runs when entering a view or range and
  when data changes from no timed occurrence to timed work; it does not reset
  on ordinary renders or later event updates, preserving manual scrolling.
- Kept empty and all-day-only ranges at the page's current top position rather
  than scrolling the `00:00` row to the viewport edge, so their heading and
  controls remain available.
- Recorded the behavior in the accepted product design contract and added the
  issue/task linkage in Autonomos.

## Evidence

- The integrated Herald browser project `Calendar review workspace` contains
  six publications at 15:54–15:57 UTC. Switching to Week positioned the
  dashboard scroll container at 341px (the page's maximum at that viewport),
  with the six events visible in the first screenful; switching to Day kept
  all six events in the time grid.
- The empty-project browser scenario confirms Week retains the `00:00` first
  slot and does not move the dashboard scroll container from zero.

## Follow-up

Review the adaptive anchor with schedules whose first timed publication is
early morning, late night, or on a different day of the Week range. If those
cases remain legible, the next calendar decision should focus on the reading
model for dense multi-day work rather than adding a universal working-hours
default.
