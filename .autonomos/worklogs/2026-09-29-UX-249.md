# UX-249 — Interrupted composition recovery and route follow-ups

- Date: 2026-09-29
- Status: Complete; recovery checks passed; older browser journeys were updated to wait for the new form-readiness boundary
- Related issue: ISSUE-143

## Evidence and decision

The previous new-publication recovery alert occupied the full page width above
the narrower composer, repeated its message as a title plus explanation, and
used a bordered alert. It remained visible after the operator began editing.
While it remained unresolved, both local and server autosave were suspended,
and selecting Restore replaced every current composition field. Starting to
type was therefore an implicit conflict with no safe resolution.

We made recovery a deliberate fork before authoring. The form is inert until
the server draft check completes and until the operator either restores or
discards an available draft. This avoids a race in which new input can arrive
before a server-side recovery is discovered and avoids silently replacing
current text. The recovery cue now sits within the composer's 6xl measure, uses
one draft icon and a short excerpt of the actual recoverable content, and uses
the established raised surface and shadow rather than an alert border. It has
one short title and two explicit actions; no explanatory sentence is needed.
The existing local/server precedence, media, target, and schedule restoration
logic remains intact.

The focused browser journey verifies server-backed recovery, the blocked form,
restore, subsequent editing, discard, and narrow-screen non-overflow. A second
journey forces server autosave failure and verifies local-only recovery. The
desktop and 390 px recovery states were rendered and visually inspected in the
isolated fixture. No draft was changed in the owner's browser session.

The first complete repository gate reached 39/40 browser scenarios. A later
trace established that older journeys sometimes used Playwright's programmatic
fill while the new form was still `inert` during the server recovery check.
React discarded that input, leaving validation to reject an empty composition.
The affected journeys now wait for `aria-busy=false` before authoring and for
creation to settle before opening Queue. The two new recovery scenarios passed
in the complete run. ISSUE-143 and UX-249 are closed. The final repository
gate, including 40 primary browser scenarios and 2 password-recovery scenarios,
passed after the subsequent UX-250 changes.

## Adjacent interface review

The authenticated published manual publication and a media-only draft share a
heavy edit-form hierarchy despite different jobs. The published state repeats
its content in both source and preview, promotes revision and source format,
and carries a separate lifecycle strip and destination strip. The media-only
draft appears as “Media · 2” in Queue but “Untitled publication” on its detail
page, with a large empty body editor above the media. ISSUE-144 / UX-250 now
tracks a state-led publication detail recomposition.

Queue repeats project/time-zone framing, shows a zero-count Scheduled filter,
and gives published history and actionable drafts similar row weight. ISSUE-145
/ UX-251 tracks a focused triage pass while preserving all filters and history.
The unsupported-only Performance page shows one unavailable-statistics row and
little else; ISSUE-146 / UX-252 asks for a provider-state comparison before a
route or navigation choice. The Channels page repeats a disconnected LinkedIn
destination and its connection prompt, with a zero-connection counter and two
connection affordances; this evidence is attached to existing ISSUE-063 /
UX-244 rather than duplicated as a new task.
