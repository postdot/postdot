# WEB-159 — Reclaim empty Week/Day space

- Status: complete
- Date: 2026-09-12
- Task: WEB-159
- Issue: ISSUE-083
- Related tasks: WEB-157, WEB-158

## Owner direction

The calendar must foreground scheduled work without throwing away valid
overnight times. Starting every row at `00:00` with the same height made a
publication around `15:54` appear below the first useful viewport, while the
time gutter and repeated gaps consumed space that could have supported the
content. The solution should adapt to empty versus occupied slots, not invent
an arbitrary working-hours boundary.

## What changed

- Reduced the empty time-slot baseline to `2rem` and removed the second nested
  minimum-height declaration that had added the slot baseline on top of the
  cell padding. Empty hours remain fully rendered and clickable for creation.
- Kept occupied slots in normal flow, so their height is the greater of the
  compact baseline and their stacked publication content. Dense blocks still
  expand naturally and never get clipped by a fixed hour row.
- Reduced the time gutter from `4.5rem` to `3.5rem`, tightened label padding,
  and reduced the time-grid gaps to 2px. The seven-column Week surface keeps
  its bounded minimum width; Day remains fluid.
- Added an E2E assertion that a slot containing six simultaneous publications
  is taller than an empty slot, preserving the intended content-driven layout.

## Follow-up

Evaluate row/column resizing separately with three concrete use cases: a dense
single hour, a week with long channel names, and a user who needs overnight
scheduling. Any resize control should define its scope, keyboard/touch model,
and persistence before it is added to the calendar surface.
