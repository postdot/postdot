# PERF-615 — PostgreSQL workflow plans and sustained admission

## Scope

Follow-up to ISSUE-195 and DB-614. Preserve SQL-backed durable execution,
transactional claims, system-actor scope, leases, cadence checks and effect fencing.
No new runtime dependency, extension, migration or concurrency-policy change.
Changes are isolated on `perf/postgres-worker-plans`, based on `f86ae0923`.

## Cause and intervention

The first trace of the real executor attributes approximately 16 seconds to
claiming a job. Controlled `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` then reproduces the problem
with 10,000 due jobs and no statistics after bulk insertion. Unlike the earlier
DB-614 probe, this experiment disables automatic table statistics **only in its
disposable database** so every initial plan sees the same state. Normal operation
and the sustained worker probe retain automatic maintenance.

The original queries underestimate eligible rows and choose a nested-loop plan
that rejects 99,990,000 job/target row pairs. Both claim and next-event discovery
take about 20 seconds. No JIT section appears, and disabling JIT leaves the delay:
this cliff comes from the join plan, not JIT compilation. Explicit ANALYZE brings
those original query executions down to approximately 174 ms / 147 ms.

PostgreSQL now validates publisher presence and the retained target/profile through
correlated bounded EXISTS lookups. `LIMIT 1 OFFSET 0` intentionally prevents those
checks from being flattened into the large candidate join. The same predicates
and atomic UPDATE remain; SQLite retains its original join strategy. The cadence
expression uses PostgreSQL GREATEST directly only for operands known non-null
(required `jobs.run_at`, coalesced rule/history values), avoiding duplicate null
checks and correlated subqueries. General-purpose null-propagating SQL helpers are
unchanged, as is SQLite's cadence expression/index compatibility.

With the revised SQL, the initial-statistics claim/discovery executions take
approximately 175 ms / 168 ms, with no quadratic join rejection. This is about
115 times faster for the claim **in this controlled fixture**, not a production
throughput multiplier. Planning falls from about 90 ms / 69 ms to 3 ms / 4 ms.

The fix does not eliminate all planning cost. With fresh statistics and JIT on,
the revised claim/discovery executions are about 214 ms / 228 ms; with JIT off,
165 ms / 152 ms. The planner's increased cost estimate can enable JIT and add
roughly 33–62 ms of compilation here. We keep the installation's JIT policy;
operator-wide tuning is not necessary to remove the multi-second cliff. Deep
backlogs still require scanning eligible work and remain materially more expensive
than small queues. More channels/projects and real cadence history need separate
profiling before drawing broader sizing conclusions.

[Compact plans and measurements](2026-11-07-PERF-615-plans.json) preserve timings,
estimates, dominant nodes and buffer counts. The raw plan trees are reproducible:

```sh
POSTDOT_TEST_POSTGRES_URL=postgres://... bun scripts/benchmark-workflow-plans.ts
```

The command captures current store SQL, rolls back every explained UPDATE and
compares initial/analyzed statistics with JIT on/off on identical synthetic data.
Run the same command against the parent implementation for the control. Page
caches are not reset; EXPLAIN includes instrumentation overhead, so these timings
must not be substituted for full job latency or throughput.

## Sustained supported admission

The new `scripts/benchmark-worker-admission.ts` uses the actual API worker and
scheduler, real manual-store publication creation, credential preparation and
persistence. An injected provider returns synthetic success after 100 ms, with no
HTTP call or real credentials. Four projects rotate arrivals; a separate single
project case measures the one-effect-per-project ceiling. The existing global
ceiling remains two projects. Each case owns a fresh database, refreshes statistics
before arrivals, and keeps automatic maintenance enabled. There is no warmup.

Arrivals follow a fixed schedule for 30 seconds. Queue wait runs from intended
offer time to provider start, including producer insertion lag. Sequential producer
writes bound outstanding insertion work; arrival duration and producer-lag p95
expose any missed offered rate. The probe records starts/finishes, actual concurrent
projects, durable job states and the backlog at the end of arrivals, then drains.
Completion during arrivals counts synthetic provider returns; final job states
verify persistence after worker shutdown.

```sh
POSTDOT_TEST_POSTGRES_URL=postgres://... bun scripts/benchmark-worker-admission.ts --seconds 30 --projects 4 --rate 8 --effect-ms 100
```

[Aggregated admission reports](2026-11-07-PERF-615-admission.json) include one-second
timelines and every final job state count. Wait p95 includes the drain phase.

| Projects | Offered/s | Engine | Provider returns at 30 s | Outstanding at 30 s | Final result | Wait p95 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 8 | SQLite | 240/240 | 0 | all succeeded by 30.0 s | 4 ms |
| 4 | 8 | PostgreSQL | 240/240 | 0 | all succeeded by 30.0 s | 54 ms |
| 4 | 16 | SQLite | 479/480 | 1 | all succeeded by 30.1 s | 5 ms |
| 4 | 16 | PostgreSQL | 479/480 | 1 | all succeeded by 30.1 s | 356 ms |
| 4 | 24 | SQLite | 582/720 | 138 | all succeeded by 37.1 s | 6.68 s |
| 4 | 24 | PostgreSQL | 157/720 | 563 | scheduler failure; 562 pending, 1 running | 3.97 s* |
| 4 | 24, repeat | PostgreSQL | 485/720 | 235 | all succeeded by 43.9 s | 13.22 s |
| 1 | 16 | SQLite | 291/480 | 189 | all succeeded by 49.4 s | 18.41 s |
| 1 | 16 | PostgreSQL | 243/480 | 237 | all succeeded by 58.3 s | 26.98 s |

*The failed run's wait excludes the 563 jobs that never reached the provider;
it is not better latency than the successful repeat. Outstanding includes work
in flight and producer lag. In these runs insertion lag p95 stays below 10 ms,
and all arrival windows remain approximately 30 seconds, so producer backpressure
does not hide the overload. No admission violation occurs: at most two projects
reach the provider concurrently, at most one in the single-project case.

At 8/s and 16/s across four projects, these short runs keep up. At 24/s, arrivals
exceed service capacity and waits grow. A 100 ms effect alone implies an ideal
upper bound of 20/s across two slots or 10/s for one project; credential preparation,
claims, fencing, persistence and scheduling reduce the actual rate. The single-
project results demonstrate why adding idle projects or more HTTP replicas does
not increase one project's concurrency. These are local synthetic measurements,
not a safe production rate, a user-count limit or a provider-capacity guarantee.

One PostgreSQL overload run also exposes a different failure: transaction retries
can exhaust, after which the scheduler's existing failure path stops execution.
The last rejected database statement in the server log promotes a publication
from scheduled to ready. A second current-code run succeeds. The parent-code
reference at the same rate also succeeds (477 returns at 30 s, all 720 by 44.5 s).
These samples establish an intermittent failure, not its probability or whether
the new plan changes that probability. Retry count, jitter, serializable isolation
and scheduler error policy are unchanged. No claim is made that ordinary backlog
growth always drains safely on PostgreSQL. This recovery requirement is tracked
separately as **ISSUE-196**, with deterministic fault injection before/after the
effect boundary needed before selecting and implementing a recovery policy.

The run machine is an AMD Ryzen 7 5800X, Bun 1.4.4, PostgreSQL 18.6 in a local
container with fsync/synchronous_commit/JIT on. SQLite is on disk with WAL/FULL.
No other qualification suites ran during the sustained cases. No real provider,
media transfer, HTTP admission, replies, first comments, long-duration soak,
cross-region deployment or process crash is represented.

## Validation

- Full persistence suite: SQLite **369 passed, 6 skipped**; PostgreSQL **385
  passed, 4 skipped**. Skips retain their existing engine-specific scope.
- API workflow module: **145 passed on each engine**, including admission,
  provider execution, credential preparation, retries and ambiguous outcomes.
- New eligibility regression passes on both engines: disabled versus superseded
  retained profiles, publisher absence, destination revision and a final first
  claim after re-enabling eligibility. Existing cadence, claim and fencing tests
  pass without weakened assertions.
- `bun run check:quick` passes: formatting, lint, spelling and types.
- No local browser matrix: no UI or transport change; the grouped PR runs the
  repository CI matrix. Performance probes ran separately from qualification.

## Follow-up

The owned temporary PostgreSQL container was stopped and removed after qualification.

Resolve ISSUE-196's background recovery policy with deterministic fault injection.
Do not introduce another queue service or increase project concurrency on the
basis of these measurements. Broader production sizing still needs realistic
provider/media work and a sustained soak on the intended deployment hardware.
