## Projects and people

Mixpost has two different self-hosted propositions. Lite is the free, MIT-licensed
[public repository](https://github.com/inovector/mixpost). Pro is a commercial product
with more providers, workspaces, collaboration and automation. Keeping the editions
separate matters when you choose what your team will use.

Postdot includes workspaces, projects, groups, invitations and project roles in the
same application. A workspace can contain several projects, each with its own posts,
media and collaborators. Workspace owners and administrators manage the organization;
project owners, editors and viewers work within the access granted to their projects.

A destination can serve more than one project. Attach the same channel to a product
launch and an events project, reuse its connection, and coordinate the occupied times.
The [shared-channel workflow](/docs/shared-channels/) keeps channel management separate
from permission to publish through it. You can keep campaigns distinct while sharing
a company account.

Mixpost Pro's [workspace model](https://mixpost.app/features) separates brands and
clients, with users, settings and roles per workspace. That is useful for teams that
want each client in an isolated publishing environment.

## Manual publishing and review

Postdot gives destinations you publish to yourself the same place in the plan as
connected accounts. A manual channel stores the prepared publication, media and date;
you complete the submission in the destination and record the result in Postdot.
You can plan a community announcement alongside a scheduled social post without
maintaining another calendar.

Mixpost Pro has strengths in formal review: its [collaboration features](https://mixpost.app/features)
include approval workflows, review comments and an activity timeline. Pro also adds
an engagement inbox and broader reporting. These are useful reasons to buy its
commercial edition when review and engagement management drive your work.

Postdot provides draft review in Studio and explicit scheduling with project-scoped
publishing permissions. If you need Mixpost's approval chains or client-facing
commercial features, compare those tasks directly with your team's process.

## Automation and n8n

Postdot includes CLI, HTTP API, MCP and agent skills. Your agent can prepare drafts
that you review in Studio, and scripts can work with the same posts and publications.
The [n8n community node](/docs/n8n/) lists projects and channels, creates unscheduled
text drafts and reads publication state on self-hosted n8n.

Mixpost's [agent guide](https://mixpost.app/agents) explicitly includes MCP, CLI and
REST API in self-hosted Pro and Enterprise, and excludes agent access from Lite.
Its [API reference](https://docs.mixpost.app/api/) also points to an n8n community node.
The difference is what the edition includes, rather than whether Mixpost can work
with agents at all.

For a pipeline that turns release notes into drafts, Postdot lets the writing tools,
workflow and editorial review stay separate. An external tool prepares the content;
Postdot keeps the destination versions and their publication state.

## Platforms, formats and languages

Mixpost's [edition comparison](https://mixpost.app/pricing) lists Facebook Pages, X
and Mastodon for Lite. Its paid editions expand the catalog to destinations such as
Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, TikTok, Pinterest and Bluesky.

Postdot includes connections for Bluesky, Mastodon, DEV.to, Discord, X, LinkedIn,
Telegram, Facebook and Instagram. That gives a team needing LinkedIn, Bluesky and
Discord a different free-software option from Mixpost Lite's three listed networks.
Use the [provider capability guide](/docs/providers/provider-capabilities/) to match
formats to the posts you plan. In particular, Postdot's Instagram connection accepts
one JPEG; paid Mixpost supports additional social and video workflows.

Postdot's Studio supports English, French, Spanish and German. Mixpost lists
multilingual support in Pro and presents a [broader language selection](https://mixpost.app/features),
including per-user preferences. A multilingual team should compare the required
languages as well as the edition that supplies them.

## Cost and operation

Postdot distributes the application under AGPL-3.0-only, with its project access,
provider connections and integration surfaces together. Mixpost Lite uses MIT.
Mixpost's paid source and commercial terms are a separate offering from that public
Lite codebase.

The [self-hosted pricing page](https://mixpost.app/pricing) lists Pro at $299 and
Enterprise at $1,199, before VAT, with one year of updates and continued use of the
purchased version. Enterprise adds features for operating a subscription business.
Mixpost also offers a separate [Cloud service](https://mixpost.app/cloud).

Postdot lets you choose SQLite or PostgreSQL and local or S3-compatible media
independently. Mixpost provides Docker, standalone PHP and Laravel installation
paths. If you already operate Laravel applications, that integration may fit your
infrastructure well.

## Try your publishing workflow

Start with the [Postdot installation guide](/docs/installation/). Create the projects
you work on, invite collaborators and attach their channels. Try one connected
publication and one [manual destination](/features/manual-publishing/), then connect
your agent or n8n workflow when you want to prepare drafts from another tool.
