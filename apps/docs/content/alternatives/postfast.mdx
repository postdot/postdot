## Projects and people

PostFast is a hosted publishing service. Postdot is an application you run yourself,
with workspaces containing projects and reusable destinations. Those choices affect
how you organize people and campaigns as much as who operates the server.

In Postdot, create separate projects for a product, an event or a client. Each keeps
its own posts, media and collaborators. Attach a workspace-owned channel to the
projects that need to use it, so they share one connection and a coordinated schedule.
Members can see occupied times on a shared destination while project permissions
control access to the underlying content.

PostFast's [workspace design](https://postfa.st/features/post-approvals) isolates
accounts, calendars, drafts and members by client. It also lets you invite clients
for review or send connection links for them to authorize their social accounts.
That suits a service-led workflow in which each client's publishing lives in a
separate environment.

Postdot manages people through workspace membership, groups, invitations and project
owner/editor/viewer roles. PostFast documents Admin, Editor and Client roles with
per-user overrides. Invited reviewers occupy user slots in their PostFast workspace,
which matters when you choose a plan for a client roster.

## Manual publishing and review

In Postdot, a publication can be due for a person to complete. Add a manual channel,
prepare its copy and media, and schedule the work in the same calendar as automated
posts. Publish through the destination's own interface and record the result.
This keeps community submissions and other human-finished work with the campaign.

PostFast's [approval workflow](https://postfa.st/features/post-approvals) is a strong
reason to choose it for structured team sign-off. Only posts in the Approved state
publish. Its permissions can hold a particular editor's work for review, including
posts created through integrations.

Postdot's draft-and-review workflow uses Studio and explicit scheduling, with project
access controlling publishing actions. A team requiring an enforced approval gate
should evaluate that requirement alongside its planning and access model.

## Automation and n8n

Both tools expose a REST API and MCP. Postdot also supplies a CLI and agent skills.
You can ask an agent to prepare a publication, inspect it in Studio and schedule
its destination versions when they are ready. The [automation guide](/docs/automation/)
provides concrete examples for your own instance.

Postdot's [n8n node](/docs/n8n/) focuses on draft preparation for self-hosted n8n:
list projects and channels, create an unscheduled text draft, then read its saved
publication state. That fits a workflow in which an event in your other tools
starts an editorial review.

PostFast's [verified n8n integration](https://postfa.st/api-integrations/n8n) runs on
n8n Cloud and self-hosted n8n. Its node covers posting, account discovery, analytics
and media-upload URLs. PostFast also offers hosted connectors for compatible AI
clients. Choose it when those managed connections and the broader native node
operations are what you need.

## Platforms, formats and languages

Postdot's provider connections cover Bluesky, Mastodon, DEV.to, Discord, X, LinkedIn,
Telegram, Facebook and Instagram. Manual channels and custom HTTP publishers extend
the kinds of destination you can keep in the plan. Studio supports English, French,
Spanish and German.

PostFast advertises [eleven connected networks](https://postfa.st/integration),
including YouTube, TikTok, Pinterest, Threads and Google Business Profile. Those
are useful differences for a video-heavy or local-business workflow. Postdot's
[provider guide](/docs/providers/provider-capabilities/) details its current formats;
its Instagram connection is a single-JPEG publication, rather than a replacement
for a full Reel or Story workflow.

For analytics and engagement, PostFast offers reporting and a Social Inbox.
Postdot keeps saved provider observations for Bluesky, Mastodon and DEV.to, with
conversation snapshots for Bluesky and Mastodon. Match those surfaces to the
accounts you manage and the questions you need to answer.

## Cost and operation

PostFast bundles account, workspace, user and queue allowances into
[subscription plans](https://postfa.st/pricing). At the review date, monthly billing
lists Starter at €15 with four social accounts, one workspace and one user, and
Creator at €29 with twelve accounts, four workspaces and five users per workspace.
It prices plans rather than charging a separate fee for each seat.

Postdot's application has no software seat or account tiers. You operate the
instance and choose its infrastructure, backups and provider applications. Its
project roles, shared destinations, four Studio languages and automation interfaces
are included in the same AGPL-licensed application.

Choose SQLite or PostgreSQL for data and local or S3-compatible storage for media.
PostFast operates its managed service for you. The useful cost comparison includes
both the subscription you would choose and the infrastructure you want to maintain.
